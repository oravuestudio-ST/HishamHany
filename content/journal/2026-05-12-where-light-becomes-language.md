A photograph is a sentence written in light. Before I touch a camera, I read the room
the way an editor reads a draft — looking for what to cut.

## Subtraction first

Most images fail from excess, not absence. A campaign frame earns its authority by what
it refuses: the stray reflection, the second light that flatters nothing, the prop that
asks for attention it hasn't earned. I start every shoot by removing, not adding.

## Direction over decoration

A car, a garment, a face — each carries its own grammar. My work is to find the line of
that grammar and follow it, so the viewer reads the image in the order I intend. Light is
the punctuation. Shadow is the pause.

When the frame finally resolves, it should feel inevitable — as if it could not have been
composed any other way.
